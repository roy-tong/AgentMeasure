Your AI support bill counts “resolutions” it attested itself

2026-09-19 · about the AMS-1 settlement-statement standard · get your bill checked, free →

The invoice line reads 8,012 resolutions × $0.99. Or × $1.50, or × $2.00 — Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI, and Salesforce all now bill AI support this way, and the model is spreading.

Here is the question nobody on the invoice answers: who confirmed that each of those 8,012 was a resolution?

What “resolved” quietly includes

Across vendors, the resolution counter typically includes outcomes only the vendor's own system marked:

  • Assumed resolutions — the customer stopped replying, so the conversation closed “resolved.” Community threads full of operators discovering this the hard way are not hard to find.
  • Deflections — counted as outcomes on some plans even when the customer never confirmed anything.
  • Confirmation-click resolutions — the customer clicked “Yes, this helped” on a prompt. One Zendesk community commenter put it plainly: “Charging an autoresolution simply because an end user clicks Yes is broken.”
  • Escalations that still got counted — the AI handed off to a human, after the meter had already ticked.

None of this is hidden. It is in the pricing docs, in the definitions, in the product's own semantics. The problem is structural: the vendor's AI marks the outcome, the vendor's meter counts it, and the vendor bills it. We checked twenty vendors in this space — not one publishes a dispute process for the resolution count on your invoice.

One distinction does most of the work: who attested the outcome?

An outcome the vendor attested and an outcome your customer attested are different facts. Blending them into one number is how a bill becomes unarguable.

The two-line statement (AMS-1)

Our open settlement-statement standard refuses to blend. A statement carries exactly two lines, computed from your own export:

Tier 1 — everything the vendor's OWN published rules would bill
        (their resolutions + their assumed resolutions)
Tier 2 — only resolutions an affected party actually confirmed

The gap between the two lines is the disputed class.

Tier 1 leads the negotiation for a simple reason: the vendor cannot argue with their own rules — only with the data, which is yours. Tier 2 is renewal leverage, not a dispute line. The two are never blended into a single “true number,” because a blended number is exactly the kind of claim both sides can reject.

And the discipline that makes it hard to attack: anything your export cannot prove is listed as UNPROVABLE and removed from any claim — with the missing evidence named, so the vendor can supply it or credit it. A statement that guesses zero rows is a statement that loses.
